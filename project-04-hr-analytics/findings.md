## HR Attrition Analysis —  Key Business Findings

### Finding 1: Attrition Rate is Above Industry Average
The company has a 16.12% attrition rate, slightly above the 
industry average of 10-15%. With 1,470 employees, this means 
237 people have left — each replacement costing the company 
months of salary in hiring and training costs.

### Finding 2: Overtime is the Biggest Attrition Driver
Employees working overtime leave at 30.53% — three times higher 
than the 10.44% rate for normal-hours employees. This suggests 
burnout is a critical retention issue that management must address 
immediately.

### Finding 3: Young Employees and Sales Roles are High Risk
Employees who left averaged 33.6 years old versus 37.5 for those 
who stayed. Sales Executives and Laboratory Technicians show the 
highest attrition by role. The business should investigate 
compensation and growth opportunities in these roles specifically.

### Recommendation
Reduce mandatory overtime, review compensation for Sales and 
Lab roles, and create structured career development programs 
targeting employees under 35.
